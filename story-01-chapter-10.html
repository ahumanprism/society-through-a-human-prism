I did not see him as he had promised. At that time, I kept looking for his motorbike’s roar, but nothing occurred. Then I was told by his mother that due to the unwilled training, he was unable to apply for leave. I was displeased, looking at the ivy. For a moment, I did intend to break it into pieces. But if I had done so, would I have become no different from that man?

Today was another birthday of me. Two years ago, I remembered we were together celebrating my day. This year was not probably about us, but me myself. I understood that life without him was like that of my default back then. Admittedly, I was very happy with the two previous birthdays. In those celebrations, I felt as if I was the happiest person on Earth, as if suffering were no longer there, leaving only jubilation and our hearty laughs.

He might return by the end of this year. “Soon…” I murmured, my hands caressing the ivy’s leaves. That rage gave more credit to how bad I was. The way I felt did not validate the way I should vent my negative feelings on someone or something. It would be easier if they were positive ones. Overall, I got so impulsive, worried, and childish. It must be certainly true that I was childish, as only children behave so impulsively like that.

But did I deserve to be justified for my impulsiveness? I meant was it not the heritage left by that man? He must have been proud of me and his abusive result. No. That is, I was a monster. He was not intrinsically a human being. The way he behaved, the way he spoke, the way he existed, all were but monstrous and inhumane. Regardless of how much I disdained him, I could not deny this monster’s guise.

After school, I stopped by the supermarket to buy some stuff like fags, beers, milk, something like that. Before going home, I had passed the bakery and seen a birthday cake that had the same pattern as what I had given him. At first, I wondered if I should buy one to honour my awesome existence, but I decided to turn away. No one was here with me, so what on earth did I have to be gimcrack?

Earlier, whilst I was in the Early Modern Literature class, Henry crumpled a paper and threw it to me, signalling me to open it. As it turned out, he wanted to probe me out, asking if I was free this afternoon. With my personality, I apparently straightforwardly replied that I had some revision to do and had no time in the following days. I intentionally pre-empted his scheme in order to evade his alternative options. The truth, nonetheless, came as a complete revelation to me. Somehow he knew that today was my birthday, and he leveraged the Literature club for his intention. I did not care if he kept fabricating or concocting stories. It was always either Pedro or no one else.

I walked in my room and realised how I became so reckless with my studies. Back in the first year, I felt like a fresh student, actively engaging in lectures and discussions at the club. But I did not know why I had become so unmotivated lately. Was this existentialism people often speculate about online?

Or it might be the fallout from writing, that writers often carry a third eye of sensitivity. Since they sense everything and everyone in a more special and delicate way than laymen, they go through mental torment more greatly. I heard and read about Virginia Woolf that she attempted to take her own life, because she had been enduring unspeakable and inexplicable feelings. Or in those days, the notions of depression and mental health issues were not actually common, so her lonely suffering was ineluctable.

In my reckoning, no matter how hard we try, the idea of death remains. Only because it transitions from a stranger to a close friend. Death is horrible to many. I had always thought of it. It haunted me so often that I had to pinch myself or punch myself in the head to stop those life-and-death thoughts. I remembered that I was once caught self-healing by that man, and he was about to take me to some so-called spirit medium to see if I was possessed or not, but my mother spoke up against him, saying that I was knackered because of the heavy schoolwork. That was the one and only first time I had felt the humanity left in him.

This day last year was so much fun. At that time, Pedro offered to celebrate my birthday, but I feigned illness and wanted to be alone. He, evidently, brought medicine and a birthday cake to my doorstep. I was very happy, of course, but was also afraid that this play of me might teeter on the brink of being cut short. A moment later, he arrived, and I had to wrap the blanket around myself to make me guiltless. I told him that he was too headstrong to notice the falling rain. Not only did he not mind it, but he also said that he must come so as not leave me alone. “If not now, then when?” he said, a line that still lingered on my memory. I smiled and told him to put away his deft articulation. After that, we played Monopoly and got into bed until late afternoon.

At around ten, I got a call from Pedro, and he said that he wanted to see me out front. He added that if I was not available, there was no need to see him. I was dumbstruck for a moment, tears welling in my eyes. I told him to wait for me, my lips quivering, and pulled myself together with haste to find out what the fuck was happening. Back then, I was showering, so I clearly did not hear his engine. He, in the end, came back, inadvertently and in an unlooked-for manner. I quickly dried my tearful eyes and opened the window to see him. In that very instant, he looked like a knight, a caballero, returned home from the battlefield, and I was his betrothed crying tears of happiness.

He followed me to get inside, and both of us did not say a thing. The formality in the room heightened intensely. He was sitting on my creaky bed, whilst I was turning to the window to avoid his eyes. It felt as if we were performing mime for a while when he, all of a sudden, sat up and rushed to hug me tightly. His body was so warm and huge that I felt small in his chest. Each heartbeat, each wisp of warmth, each breath of him brought be from the angsty hell back to life. I patted on his back and said: “It’s alright. You’re alright.”

“I’m sorry,” he sobbed.

“Don’t be. You did nothing wrong.” I took him to the bed and continued reassuring: “It’s all gone. I don’t mind at all. Are you tired, or should I…?”

“No, Anthony. Don’t do anything. Just stay with me. I really miss you.”

“Okay, okay. I’m not going anywhere. Don’t you worry.”

He must have felt like he himself committed some crime, and now he was begging for mercy before his divine god. Witnessing his penitence, I got more broken-hearted. But I was very joyful, because he was fine and even more mature. He got a military buzz cut that was super cool. When he leaned on me, my face was tickled by his pointed hair. His moustache was unchanged, but his hands got more calluses. I caressed his hair and said that he ought to stay here, as it was rather late.

“I don’t wanna sleep,” he drawled. “I want to talk to you for a little bit.”

“You drink alcohol?” Its wafting smell almost made me want to puke. I did love alcoholic drinks, but this smell caused me to be bewildered. “Stay here. I’ll make you a warm lemon water with honey to help you sober up.”

“Anthony, please…” He gripped my wrist, to the extent that even if I exerted myself, I could barely resist his burly body.

“Alright. Let’s talk then.”

“I didn’t mean to… upset you. I just wanted you to… forget about me.”

“Forget about you? I don’t understand.”

“I know that… while I wasn’t here, you’d feel… very lonely. So I upset you… on purpose, so that we…”

“You idiot.” I tapped lightly on his head and said: “Why would I feel lonely? You told me you’d keep in touch with me.”

“I know… but in the long run…”

“You were more worried than I was. Didn’t you tell me to focus on the present? Why did you break your word then?”

“I’m sorry…”

“Okay. I’ve already been upset enough. If you want me to forgive you, that’s fine. Give me a birthday present then. Not having any present for my birthday is a little bit ungentlemanly , you know.” I sighed with amusement.

“That’s for sure…” He then pulled out a pocket watch and gave it to me. “It was my father. It belonged to my grandfather, but he handed down to my father. And now, I give it to you.”

“Something this precious, how can I…?”

“Because… you deserve it, hermano.”

“Thank you, Pedro!”

“You haven’t met me for ages. Don’t you miss me?”

“Of course not.” I scanned the watch and noticed that there was the letter H on it. It must have been his grandfather who carved his father’s name on the watch as a family heirloom. He completely broke my heart just by his pitiful mien. I inwardly implored God to let us live in this blissful moment, whatever the cost to myself might be. “Was the training tough?”

“Just normal.”

“You’re lying.”

“I’m being serious.”

“You’re a bad liar.”

Then he smirk. It was still that smirk. I thought it looked plain, but it took me quite a long time to see it again. He was wearing the navy blue uniform imbued with his body odour. After that, he said he wanted to take a shower, but it was sort of late, so I told him to shower tomorrow. I rummaged through the wardrobe and found an outfit that suited him, but the biggest size of my clothes seemed not to make him comfortable.

I pretended that I had some class the next day, so I had to go to bed soon, thus successfully tricking him to go to sleep. I conceded my bed to him and stealthily sat at the desk thinking of what had just happened. I put the watch in the drawer with care and opened the novel to carry on my literature journey.

At the moment, he was sleeping and even snoring every now and again. I came to him, pulling the blanket around himself, and stroked his hair. “Good night, Pedro,” I said, smiling.
