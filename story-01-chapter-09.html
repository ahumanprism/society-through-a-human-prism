09 August 2009
Back to Literature

I remembered that it was 7 January 2009, I was taking an exam at school. Coincidentally, that very day was his departure for the army. Though he messaged me to come to bid him farewell if I lazed about, his messages ended up getting buried because of my tight schedule with academic stuff. That night I could barely fall asleep with two trails of tears and an unending yearning for him.

I remembered that it was 24 March 2009, that feeling hit me again—the first days knowing him and the existence of that voluable mouth. It had been quite a long time since I met him. At times, I, as per usual, went to that beach in an absent-minded manner, where pieces of memories of us were disorientating, like footage reshowing the glorious past of us. “Glorious…” I whispered. I then lit a fag and quietly sat on the sand, gazing at the falling dusk. “If I had made it to see him the last time, I would have been…”

I sometimes swung by his home to talk to his family. After that explosion, everyone seemed to care about me more, especially his mother. She kept asking about my studies as well as my well-being. She also said that if I wanted, I could stay in his room until he came back. I knew that this was his offer, but I really wondered if I simmered down or I was still looking for our beautiful memories and was then lost in them like the way I turned to this fag.

His sister was enraptured by my literary works, as I, on occasion, showed her my short stories. I, as a matter of fact, was unsure if my writing style suited her naivety, but she did not think so. On the other day, whilst I was having lunch and thinking of him, she came to me and said: “You write so well, Anthony. I want to be a writer like you.” I just patted on her head and said that she ought to focus on studying subjects at school and take writing as a pastime, not framing herself but letting it flow with the surrounding currents. Then, words could totally live on in readers’ hearts.

I turned to the distant sea and took a dip in spite of the burning fag in my hand. Since it was autumn, the temperature was rather low. Whilst my body was undergoing a fever, it seemed to emit some heat to balance out the cold outside. It might sound injudicious, but whenever I felt off-colour, I felt as if I were being draped with a thick blanket. It was chill outside, but within me, there was some sort of exuding warmth. I reached out to the terns flying high above me, irrespective of the water gradually permeating my body.

I remembered that it was 7 April 2009, I celebrated his birthday alone. Though his family invited me to take a commemorative picture together, I politely declined and said that I was not feeling well. Truth to be told, I was physically and mentally exhausted. Had I come to his home with my shakiness, who would I have become? That being said, I did wish to celebrate his birthday alone. In fact, I had prepared a present before. This year, I, in particular, knit him a white jersey with his name on the back and a large number “07”. He was passionate about football, and there was a time that he and his mates achieved multiple trophies in a row during secondary school. But again, owing to the financial issue, he could not afford his dream. I sometimes watched him play football, yet he told me: “Probably I’m an adult now, so I find football a little bit strange.”

I remembered that it was 16 May 2009, in my dream, I met him again. He, in the military uniform, looked immaculately attractive and manly. He and I strolled along the beach, smoking, drinking, and talking shit like the good old days. His face, I recalled, was etched with gravitas of time. Wrinkles around the eyes and the forehead thus emerged. I teased him that he was old and was no longer virile like before. I also added that if he did not settle down soon, he would miss his manhood. He, by contrast, said nothing but looked at me with those eyes. After that, he held my hands and whispered: “Please take good care of yourself.” And he vanished, as though his existence were but a mere ephemerality. I woke up and realised that all was just a dream, not too good nor too bad to be called a nightmare. The dream was basically my unsubtle ardency for Pedro, the man I had been persistently pining for.

I remembered that it was 14 June 2009, whilst I was on my lunch break, the captain came to me and asked if I still kept in touch with Pedro. I simply nodded and smiled. He then told me that after being discharged from the military, he would come back here to continue the work. At that time, I thought why he made such an abrupt decision, because obviously if he remained in the navy, he could be promoted down the road, hence a brighter future ahead. Then I, in a mindless moment, checked my phone. There, I saw nothing since those farewell words.

I remembered that it was 24 July 2009, the blazing sunshine of summer reminded me of his figure. I wondered what he was doing at the present, if he lived well or talked nonsense with his fellows. I, from time to time, wondered if he ever thought about me. But I soon got rid of such simple-minded daydreaming. Who was he? A friend, at last. I, however, fancied that he was far more special than the young men I had met. What a shame!

This morning I had a call from his mother. She said that he would manage to go to see her in the next two weeks, and she asked if I let him say a few words with me. Anyway, it was her mother who spoke for him, so I could not turn her down but let out a perfunctory smile and said. Actually, I was very happy. I was happy to know that he lived well and, above all, he was going to see me in person, face-to-face, directly. I imagined that the confrontation was either equable or intemperate. Even so, I smiled a lot, my mind relieved by the knowledge that he was still doing good.

I stepped onto the shore and went back home. Having been completely drenched with seawater, plus the autumnal keenness, my warm body could not stand the perishing gusts of wind. I embraced myself to leaven the cold outside and inside. Sadly, tomorrow was very likely to be a rainy day. I had planned to go out to bask in his return, but it seemed not to permit me to leave the room laden with notions of him. “I miss you, Pedro,” I said to myself in a trembling voice.

Whilst I was attempting to sketch the Triton’s trumpet shell, the urge to admire literature within me grew suddenly strong. I opened my novel and went on the previous plot. He, by implication, always inspired me, helping me to finish the course of actions of this love story without his realisation. I sometimes argued that for this reason, he thought that he himself had the right to preview the story. But he was not aware of the fact that being inspiring and arbitrary was an entirely distinct matter. He might encourage me to move forward, but that did not mean that he owned the path I walked on and presumed that it was his, thus doing whatever he wanted. If so, I was no different than a brothel, letting him check in and out casually.

After having the chapter finished, I decided to meditate with a view to steadying myself. Yet, when I just closed my eyes, his face untimely came to my mind. Amidst the darkness, he was my light, guiding me to the paths that I had been very frightened to get through. He made things appear easier. There was a time that his motorbike could not function properly, and he then brought out the requisite tools prepared before to fix it. Seeing that, I was more interested in his talent. Perhaps theoretical knowledge was not his strength, but with regard to life skills, he indeed left me in the shade.

I sat up and opened the window to look around. I took a puff, drank a beer, and nibble on some snacks. I had not watched the stars for quite some time. Since the prevalence of social media platforms, many people had a trendy liking for some sort of horoscope reading consultation like fortune tellers in my hometown. I supposed this was but a westernised version and was not the twelve zodiac animals like ours. My curiosity then led me to give it a try, and I discovered that my zodiac sign was Sagittarius and was of the Fire element. His was Taurus and was of the Wood element. These two elements, as I learned, do not often get along well. No wonder every time I was with him, I just wanted to curse as much as possible.

“You’re a twat.”

“What did you just say?” Pedro laughed.

“I said you’re a twat, a fucking moron.”

He kept laughing whilst I was fed up to the back teeth of him. Then I walked ahead, leaving him calling me behind. As it was heavily snowing in Winchester, my gait became stiff accordingly. I thought he would take me to some famous attraction on Christmas Eve, but he surprisingly took me to a house of prostitution. Furthermore, he said that he wanted to teach me to taste the ways of life when he was away. I was about to go ballistic, but because I wanted to save our faces, I decided to transfer this fury at the end of the alley.

“Wait, Anthony,” he said, with a mouth unable to shut. “I’m just joking.”

“Your joke is working now. Congratulations!”

“Come on. Don’t be so childish like that.”

“What? Childish?” I turned to him with an annoyed expression. “I’m literally speechless.”

He seized me by the wrist and said: “Alright, Anthony. I’m sorry.”

“For what?”

“For…”

“Your goodwill is not always good to me.”

“I’m just joking.”

I sighed and said: “You do know my principles.”

“I know, but it’s just…”

“It’s just you wanted to go in there, and you didn’t want to go alone, so you took me with you. Am I right?”

He shook his head and said: “No. It’s just you’ve been feeling lonely for a long time, so I wanted to give you a chance to relax.”

“You cunning, but allow me to turn down your kind offer. And if you want to relax, I’m very pleased to wait here.”

“I’m not into having sex like that.”

“Didn’t you tell me your sex life? Why are you being so suddenly picky?”

“No, they’re different. The women there are…”

I glared at him and moved on. What just happened was beyond my conception. However, I, after this chance occurrence, knew that he was not an omnivore. He must have been afraid of health issues, so he refused to seek fleshpots like this one. Compared to Henry, he was not really a rake that lulled the women he had slept with into a lingering sensual attachment. He once told me that the number of times he had sex with women counted on his fingers. He also pointed at me and laughed when figuring out that I was a virgin and had never experienced physical intimacy before. There, nevertheless, had always existed a secret about me that he could never ever imagine that women were but my friends and sisters. Men were my literal targets. By coincidence, he was the man of whom I was enamoured.
