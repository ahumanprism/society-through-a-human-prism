Pedro messaged me that he would drop by my place to chat for a bit. It had been a year since he joined the navy. He often told me what happened there, as well as his fellows. He also mentioned me in a few conversations with them. I heard that they liked me too and wanted to have a face-to-face meeting with me somewhere down the road. If that day came, I might try my best to present my friendliness like the first day I made my acquaintance at the Puerto.

My curriculum was practically complete. Only a couple more months, I had to find a company with an internship post. How was my future then? Obviously, I would be there, at the Puerto, and write literature during break time. But what about Pedro? Would he still be there? Even though he had said that he would come back, how could I nail it on? And if he was not there, what else could I do?

He arrived at six. I told him to get in and have dinner. He was not wearing the uniform, but an outfit that looked more stately than those first days we met. Verily, the disciplined environment sharpened him. His way of talking was gentler, milder, and above all, his behaviour was more refined. His mouth was now uninterested in working with utmost efficiency, and it switched to a mode of temporary retirement. This meant that it stilled worked well, but the frequency dropped considerably. He did not smile as much as before, but he acted more instead. A month ago, if I remembered rightly, he gave me a handmade necklace of seashells that looked very charming. I was excitedly enchanted but pretended to deflect suspicion.

To be fair, I understood why he treated me so well like that. He had never met someone like me before. I did not blow my own trumpet or have any devices to tempt him. I, however, was the first person giving him a sense of comfort and warmth, as a man. I was not sure if the women he had met before treated him like I did, but I bet I was the only man willing to do everything for him, and I was even willing to see him happy with someone else. I could do it.

“You look thinner. Have you been eating poorly?" he asked, enjoying my com tam.

I involuntarily touched my face and gave a faint smile. “No, I haven’t. I look normal,” I said.

He pinched my face and said: “Is this really normal? You’ve got thinner. Come shopping with me tomorrow. I’ll buy you some nutritious food.”

“You don’t have to, Pedro. I…”

“Ant, listen to me. You’ve promised to take good care of yourself. Now you look even worse than me,” he said, sighing.

“I’m fine…”

He sat up and opened the fridge to find me a small carton of milk. “Take this when you’re done eating.”

“You’re so…” I took it and said: “Thank you.”

He started to call me Ant after that reunion. At that time, I even laughed and said that the name was no different from an ant. But he told me that calling me so was dearer, lest people saw it and deemed that we were strangers. But honestly, there was barely anyone caring or thinking about what we did. He simply named me so to call me for short. I wondered how much sincerity was underlying in that endearment.

After dinner, he took me to his home. His sister, as usual, was very eager to know about my latest publication. Her mother, on the other hand, tenderly asked how I was. Everyone loved me and viewed me as their family. He once said that I was his family and that I could move here if I would love to. But I did not want to burden anyone, and he would settle down one day; therefore, doing so might possibly stand in his way. After all, I was his mere social brother.

I was lying on the bed waiting for him to shower. It had been three years since I met him. Time did pass so quickly. Everything was like in the blink of an eye. At times, I thought what had happened between us was like yesterday. Last month, whilst I was surfing the net to find ideas for his birthday present, I came across a succulent pot. Its figure was delicate and somewhat sacred. I hesitated for a while and then decided to get it as his secondary present. As it was secondary, I indeed had prepared him a primary one. I specially knit him two pairs of gloves: one for riding the motorbike and one for the wintertime. When he took the presents, he embraced me and said that I was always thoughtful.

“It’s nothing,” I replied. In truth, what I did to him was nothing to me. I never minded making efforts to cheer him up. He must have been my life, so I was diligent about cultivating it, hoping it might bring me some novel vitality, the thing which I could hardly find in anyone or anywhere else. I hoped that this life would never abandon me literally and figuratively.

He walked in and saw me lying on bed wistfully. “What’s wrong?” he asked.

I came to my senses and said: “Nothing.”

“Ant, I’m gonna go back to Spain to arrange accommodation for my mother and my sister. I’ll be back soon.”

“Alright. Take your time. You haven’t gone back to your hometown for years, have you?”

“Yeah. It’s been a long time. I’m a little bit nostalgic, you know.” He frowned and continued: “Are you happy when you’re back in your hometown?”

“Why are you asking like that?”

“I don’t know. You seem not to mention it much, so I’m a little curious.”

“I’m happy…” I sat on the fence before replying to him.

“Ant, do you remember what we compromised on?”

“Never leave anyone behind.”

“So now, look me in the eyes and tell me if I’m being left behind or not.”

He was staring at me with those adamantine eyes. Whenever I looked at them, I felt as though I were standing before some court, completely exposed, waiting for the sentence to be pronounced. He always used those eyes to see if I was being honest or went off at a tangent. Truthfully, he always tried to be a cheerful and amiable person, but he, in fact, always protected me. I was very appreciative of his good nature. But every time the night fell, my heart contracted, as if it were pinned by needles of love or were scratched by splinters of memories. I loved him, but this idea backed me into a corner of being wracked by my own thoughts. Was it because of me or was it because of the ordained fate? “You’re not being left behind,” I said, calmly.

“Really?”

“Really. I’ve been scientifically verified.”

“Alright then.” He tousled my hair and said: “Life may be elusive and unreal, but you’ve got me. I’ll make your life more secure.”

“Thank you,” I said, eyes widening. I then saw the handmade sweater I had given him and aimlessly said: “I was wondering who knit that gorgeous sweater over there.”

“Idiot Ant, of course.” He smiled and said: “Feeling smug, are you?”

“Certainly.”

“I really like it. I haven’t even worn it yet.”

“If you don’t often wear it, how can I admire the sweater’s beauty in your massive body?”

“Are you complimenting it or me now?”

“You do know the answer.”

“Alright, me then.”

“You’re deluding yourself, señor.”

Then we did puzzles together. I thought that it might take us the whole night to finish, but the pieces gradually fit in the frame, hence the picture of a dinosaur. We again followed our custom with fags and beers and shit talk about life. In a daze, I called him Caba. He said nothing but smiled and said that he loved that nickname. I was so scared that I thought he might kick me out at once, but it turned out that the reality did not put me through the ordeal. And so, during the talk, I kept calling him Caba over and over again to express my untold intimacy to him.

I was going to sleep with him tonight. The family all persuaded me to stay over, so reluctantly accepting the offer might be better. Though I nodded as a courtesy, my heart was afire with exhilaration as if I won the lottery. Even though this was not my first time sleeping with him, this was my first time sleeping with him in his own house. For a moment, I felt like his mistress.

Around half past midnight, we both could hardly fall asleep. Our eyes were glued to the ceiling, and we were listening to the ticking of the second hand. The dim moonlight outside and the stars were altogether casting light on the dark room, as though they were investigating what was happening here. But we just lay there and remained silent. I thought that silence is not really bad, for people sometimes need certain quietness from the hectic pace of life. Noise is not good for all time, and silence is not an escape either. Reconciling between the dynamic and the still is ambiperfect.

On this April Fools’ Day, I messaged him that we ought to stop talking for some time. Once the message was sent, he gave me an immediate call and asked me what the fuck was happening. I had to act cool and hold back my wicked giggle. Though I did not see him in person, I was able to picture a Pedro, confused and frightened. I, at that time, felt so guilty, but because of that, I trusted and loved him more than yesterday.

“Ant.”

“What’s wrong?”

“Do you like me?”

“Of course.”

“Are you happy with me?”

“A little bit.”

“Just a little bit?” He turned to me, questioning.

“Sort of.”

“If you have your own family, do you still like me?”

I caressed his moustache and said: “Claro que sí, señor.”

That was our conversation, simple and random. The tipsiness soon kicked in, and we finally felt sleepy. At times, I sneaked a glance at him and smiled. And he did so, too.
